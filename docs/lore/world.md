# The world

## A dying Earth

In the near future, Earth is collapsing. Not in one blow, but in a cascade: failing harvests, drowned coasts, storms
that no longer end, each disaster making the next one worse. Governments run out of answers, then out of time. The
broadcasts still loop out into space, music and laughter and weather reports for cities that no longer exist; a pilot
far from home sometimes catches one in the static (*Echo of Earth*).

Humanity has one chance: find a habitable world, and find it before Earth fails.

## The Pathfinder

A small team of scientists did what no one else could: they built a machine that opens **stable wormholes**. They
called it the **Pathfinder**. It can't move a population yet. It can send one ship at a time, deep into galaxies no one
has charted, and it can reach back for what that ship leaves behind.

The Pathfinder runs on **Dark Matter**, an exotic substance that crystallises around unstable space. Every jump burns
it; every new technology the team builds is printed from it. So the pilots go looking for it.

## The Hub

The Pathfinder sits on an orbital laboratory above Earth, the **Hub**. It is home to the team, the hangar for the
ships, and the **Pathfinder Lab**, where recovered Dark Matter and Singularity Cores become better hulls, stronger
shields, faster dash drives and new weapons.

## The pilots

Each run is one pilot and one ship, sent through a wormhole into the first uncharted galaxy. The pilot charts a route
from jump point to jump point, fighting through whatever holds each one, and at each galaxy's heart finds a
**guardian** blocking the way on. Beat it, and the Pathfinder can open a wormhole to the next galaxy. Three galaxies
deep lies the world the long-range scans keep pointing at.

Ships in service:

- **Titan-Class Cruiser:** heavy plating and a forgiving hull. Every new pilot starts in one.
- **Stormbringer Battleship:** built around an experimental **Lightning Core** that calls strikes down on anything its
  guns hit.

## The Black Box

Every ship carries a **Black Box**. When a ship is destroyed, the Pathfinder locks onto it and pulls it home through the
wormhole: flight data, maps, combat recordings, and the Dark Matter and Cores in its hold. The ship and its improvised
equipment are lost; what was learned is not.

That is why death is progress. Every pilot who falls makes the next one stronger.

## The economy of a run

| In the game | In the world |
|---|---|
| **Scraps** | Salvage: hull plate, circuitry and parts torn from wrecks and destroyed machines. Too bulky to send home, but the ship's fabricator and the supply terminals can turn it into equipment and repairs. |
| **Dark Matter** | The Pathfinder's fuel and raw material. Harvested from crystals at unstable sites, and squeezed out of salvage when a Black Box comes home. |
| **Singularity Cores** | The collapsed heart of a guardian: a knot of folded space the Lab can study and build around. One per guardian, more in the deeper galaxies. |
| **The exit wormhole** | The Pathfinder punches through to the pilot once the area is clear enough to hold a stable jump. |
| **The Shop** | A supply terminal the Pathfinder drops ahead of the pilot before each guardian, stocked from the Lab's blueprints and fed with Scraps. |
| **The Repair station** | A rare automated dock: either a supply drop or something salvaged and coaxed back into service. |

## Open questions

- Who is on the team? A named scientist cast (a lead engineer, a mission controller who talks to the pilot) would give
  the Hub a voice. The GDD defers narrative depth until after the slice.
- How many people are left on Earth, and how long do they have? A clock would raise the stakes of every run.
- Are the pilots volunteers, clones, or crews of one? The Black Box framing works with any of them.
